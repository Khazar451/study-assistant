# Introduction to Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

Johannes Kepler published his first two laws in 1609 in Astronomia Nova, and his third law in 1619 in Harmonices Mundi.

## Kepler's First Law (The Law of Ellipses)
All planets orbit the Sun in elliptical orbits, with the Sun situated at one of the two focal points (foci). An ellipse is characterized by its semi-major axis (a) and its eccentricity (e). When eccentricity equals zero, the ellipse is a circle.

## Kepler's Second Law (The Law of Equal Areas)
A line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time. This means that a planet moves faster when it is near the Sun (at perihelion) and slower when it is farthest from the Sun (at aphelion).

## Kepler's Third Law (The Law of Harmonies)
The square of the orbital period (P) of a planet is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (a) of its orbit. Expressed mathematically:
P^2 = a^3
where P is measured in Earth years and a is measured in astronomical units (AU). This relationship demonstrates that planets further from the Sun have significantly longer orbital periods.
